Why it matters

Your credit depends on what your suppliers filed, not on what you recorded.

Input tax credit (ITC) is the GST you paid on purchases that you can set off against the GST you collect. To claim it, the supplier's invoice generally has to show up in your GSTR-2B. Every month the two sides can drift apart, and the gap is where credit gets stuck.

Your supplier files

They report their sales, including the invoice they gave you, in their own GST return.

The portal builds GSTR-2B

The GST portal collects what suppliers filed and prepares your GSTR-2B statement for the month.

This tool

You compare it with your books

Match your purchase register against GSTR-2B and see every invoice that does not line up.

You claim credit in GSTR-3B

You file your monthly return and claim ITC, knowing which invoices are safe to claim.

Doing step 3 by hand is the slow part. With hundreds of invoices, a single typo in an invoice number or a supplier who filed late is easy to miss. This tool does that step in about two minutes.

Your files

What to upload, and where to get it.

Excel or CSV, as exported. Title rows, extra sheets and two-row headers are fine. The tool finds the header row and the right sheet on its own.

Purchase register

  1. Export your purchases for the month from your accounting software, or use your own Excel sheet.
  2. Make sure each row is one invoice.
  3. Save it as .xlsx or .csv.
Columns it recognises
GSTINInvoice NoInvoice DateTaxable ValueTax AmountIGSTCGSTSGST

GSTR-2B from the portal

  1. Log in to the GST portal and open the Returns Dashboard.
  2. Choose the month and open GSTR-2B.
  3. Download the Excel file and upload it as it is.
Good to know

Portal screens change from time to time, so the steps may look slightly different. If your file has several sheets, the tool uses the one with the invoice rows (usually B2B).

Honest limits

What it does today, and what it does not do yet.

Does today

  • Matches on supplier GSTIN plus invoice number, ignoring spaces, capitals and leading zeros.
  • Treats small rounding differences as a match. You choose the limit.
  • Adds up an invoice that appears twice in one file and warns you.
  • Shows ITC at risk and gives you a six-tab Excel report.

Not yet

  • Credit notes, debit notes and amended invoices are not handled as separate sheets.
  • It does not check whether a credit is eligible or blocked under GST rules.
  • It does not file anything or connect to the GST portal.
  • It flags differences for review. A qualified CA should confirm before any claim or filing.

Questions

Before you try it.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. Your browser reads the files and does the matching on your own device. This website has no upload step and no server that receives your files.

Does it file my return or touch the GST portal?

No. It only compares two files and gives you a report. You file on the GST portal yourself.

What if my columns have different names?

The tool recognises common names such as GSTIN of Supplier, Invoice Number, Taxable Value, IGST, CGST and SGST. If a required column is missing, it tells you which one and shows the columns it could read.

What counts as a match?

The same supplier GSTIN and the same invoice number, ignoring spaces, capitals and leading zeros, with the tax within the rounding limit you set. Same supplier and same amounts with a different invoice number shows up as a possible match.

Is it free?

You can try it free right now, with no sign-up.

Can I rely on the numbers for filing?

Treat the report as a checklist of what to review. It is not tax advice, and a qualified CA should confirm results before you claim credit or file.
